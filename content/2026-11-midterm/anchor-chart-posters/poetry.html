Elements of Poetry

How poems are built and how they sound

🧱

Line & Stanza

Line = one row · Stanza = a group of lines

🎵

Rhyme & Rhyme Scheme

night / light · pattern: AABB, ABAB

🥁

Rhythm & Meter

The beat: da-DUM da-DUM

🔁

Repetition & Refrain

A refrain is a repeated line, like a chorus

🌈

Imagery & Figurative Language

Five senses + simile, metaphor, onomatopoeia

🎤

Speaker · Form

Speaker = the poem’s voice · free verse = no set rhyme

📝 Examples

“The rain begins to fall (A) / Upon the garden wall (A) / The seeds wake up below (B) / And start to stretch and grow (B)” → Rhyme scheme: AABB. Lines 1-2 rhyme (fall/wall); lines 3-4 rhyme (below/grow).
“The moon’s a lamp so bright (A) / That hangs above the town (B) / It shines all through the night (A) / Until the sun comes down (B)” → ABAB rhyme scheme (bright/night, town/down), and a metaphor: the moon IS a lamp.
Every stanza of a poem ends with “And still the poem goes on and on.” → Refrain. The repeated line ties the stanzas together and makes the poem feel endless.
READ ALOUD → LABEL THE RHYME → FIND THE IMAGES → NAME THE FEELING
Watch out! Label the rhyme scheme by SOUND, not spelling: “through” and “blue” rhyme, but “cough” and “dough” do not. A new sound always gets the next letter.
